How the lot size is calculated

Risk amount = balance × risk %. Lot size = risk amount ÷ (stop loss distance × contract size × value of the quote currency in USD). The result is rounded down to the nearest 0.01 lot so you never risk more than planned.

Example: Gold

$10,000 balance, 1% risk, entry 2650, stop loss 2640. Risk is $100. The stop is $10 away and 1 lot of Gold is 100 oz, so 1 lot loses $1,000 at your stop. Lot size = 100 ÷ 1,000 = 0.10 lots.

Things to check

  • Contract size for indices and crypto differs between brokers. Check your broker's specification and edit the contract size field if it is different.
  • For pairs where USD is the base (USDJPY, USDCAD, USDCHF) the calculator converts using your entry price. For JPY crosses it uses the USD/JPY rate you enter.
  • Spread, slippage and commission are not included. Your real loss at the stop can be slightly higher.

Why risk the same percent every trade?

A fixed risk percent keeps one bad trade from ending your account or breaking a prop firm drawdown limit. It also makes your results comparable in R multiples, which is how the QuantumTrades journal tracks performance.
